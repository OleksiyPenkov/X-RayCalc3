Main Reflectivity Chart

The large chart in the working area displays:

  • Calculated reflectivity curves (one per model, color-coded)
  • Experimental data (if loaded)
  • Best-fit curve (during and after fitting)

Interaction

  • Zoom: Click and drag to select a zoom region
  • Pan: Right-click and drag to pan
  • Reset zoom: Double-click to reset to full view
  • Scale toggle: The Linear/Log button at the right end of the Chart Info bar switches the Y-axis between linear and logarithmic
  • R min: the box under the Linear/Log button (default 1E-7; pick a decade or type a value such as 3E-8) is the reflectivity floor. It is the lower limit of the Y-axis, and more: in every calculation and every fit, each calculated point below it is raised to it before the resolution convolution, so the χ² sees the floored model. The measured data are not changed. The value is saved with the project. A good setting is one count of the measured curve after normalization, the level below which the data hold nothing.

Transparency and Draw Order v3.9.4

Where curves lie on top of each other, two settings decide what you see:

  • Transparency: open a curve's Properties dialog (double-click it in the project tree) and set Transparency, % next to its color. 0 is opaque; at 50 the curves underneath show through; 90 is the faintest a curve can be.
  • Draw order: right-click a curve in the project tree and choose Draw order > Bring to front, Bring forward, Send backward or Send to back. The curve painted last is on top. A curve you add goes on top of the others. The order of the tree and of the legend does not change.

Both are saved with the project. A project saved by an earlier version opens with every curve opaque, data curves on top of the models; an earlier version opening a newer project ignores both settings.

Residual Strip v3.9.4

Under the reflectivity curves, in a plot of its own, is the residual strip (Result > Residual strip > Show, on unless you turn it off) with log10(Rcalc / Imeas·K) at every measured point: where the model sits above the data the line is above zero, where it sits below, under it. +0.1 is a model 26 % too high, −0.3 one at half the data. The strip shows misfits the log-scale curves hide, such as a fringe field sitting high between two orders, or a stretch sitting low.

  • Same angle axis. The strip is framed apart from the reflectivity plot but is part of the same chart: zoom, pan and reset move it with the curves, and Copy as BMP/WMF, Save as graphics and Print include it. Its vertical range stays at −1 to +1.
  • One line per visible model, in the model’s color, against the linked data (see Projects). Hide a model on the legend and its line goes. Without linked data, or in wavelength mode, the strip is empty.
  • The numbers are the Fit report’s. The model is read on the measured angles (interpolated in log R) and the measured curve is taken at the solved scale K of the last calculation (Solve scale in χ²), K = 1 when the scale was anchored. The same K is used for every model, though it was solved for the model calculated last: two fits of one curve then differ by the ratio of their scales as well.
  • ±0.1 lines mark a tenth of a decade: a model 26 % above the data or 21 % below it. A band mean beyond them is worth a look.
  • Band means draws the mean of each of the Fit report’s eight bands as a step over its band, in the model’s color: the report’s “Residual by band”, read off the chart.
  • Floored points are points where the model is within 1 % of R min, or the scaled measured value is at or below it. They are not misfit, only the floor, so the line is broken there; they are drawn as gray dots, or left out when Floored points is unchecked. The band means still count them, as the report does.

The strip is recalculated after every calculation and fit, and when R min, the linked data or the visible models change; not during a fit, whose scale is solved only at the end. Its settings are saved with the application settings, not with the project.

Cursor Tracking

The Chart Info bar shows the current cursor position (X, Y coordinates) as you move the mouse over the chart.

Chart Pages

Below the main chart are tabbed sub-charts:

Thickness (H)

Bar chart showing the thickness of each layer in the structure. After fitting, this shows how layer thicknesses vary across the structure.

Roughness (σ)

Bar chart showing the interface roughness of each layer. Useful for identifying roughness trends in periodic structures.

Density (ρ)

Bar chart showing the density of each layer, with the applied density profile overlaid.

Profile

Electron density (or refractive index) profile as a function of depth into the structure. This gives a physical picture of the complete multilayer structure.

Convergence

During fitting, this chart shows the χ² value vs. iteration number. Use this to monitor convergence:

  • A steadily decreasing curve indicates good convergence
  • Plateaus may indicate local minima (shake/re-initialization helps escape these)
  • Sudden jumps up indicate a shake event

Peak Analysis

The Chart Info bar reads the active model's calculated curve, not the measured data, over the chart's visible x range. It is recomputed after every calculation and every zoom:

  • Rmax and Xmax: the highest calculated reflectivity in the visible range, and the angle (or wavelength) where it lies
  • Ri: the integrated reflectivity over the visible range, the sum of R · Δx in the chart's x unit
  • D: the model's period in Å, the summed layer thicknesses of the lowest stack with more than one period. It comes from the model, not from peak spacing.

X and Y show the cursor position in axis units while a model is active. Reflectivities below 0.01 are shown with three significant digits, e.g. 1.03E-4.
